# Translating High & Low

Thank you for translating High & Low. This guide is about how the app should *feel* in your language. It has
principles, not rules. Where it doesn't fit your language, trust your judgment and leave a short note for the next
person.

For the mechanics (which files to create and what not to touch), see `public/locales/README.md`.

---

## Who this is for

High & Low is a mood tracker for people who are often exhausted: burned out, depressed, in pain, or just running on
empty. Many will open it on a bad day, when even small things take effort, and they may do it in a few seconds with
half their attention.

Two things follow from that.

- **Nothing in the app should add to the load.** If a phrase makes someone stop and work out what it means, or feel
  that something is being asked of them, it costs them energy they may not have.
- **Nothing in the app should judge.** There are no right answers and no good or bad ways to feel. Someone who skipped a
  week, or a month, should come back to the same quiet welcome.

When you choose between two translations, ask which one a tired, fragile person would find easier and kinder to read.

---

## The feeling we're after

Aim for the voice of a calm friend who is happy to hear how you are and in no hurry.

- **Gentle and plain.** Short, ordinary words that people really use, not clinical, bureaucratic, or overly
  cheerful ones. If you would not say it out loud to someone you care about, reconsider it.
- **Unhurried.** No urgency, pressure, or scolding. Questions are invitations. Skipping is always fine, and the text
  should never suggest otherwise.
- **Warm without gushing.** Kindness matters more than enthusiasm. Avoid forced positivity, which can feel hollow or 
  even mocking to someone having a hard time.
- **Respectful of the user's pace and privacy.** The wording should treat their information as theirs: quiet and 
  personal, never watched or reported on.

---

## Two words worth extra care

Two ideas appear throughout the app, and the choice of word in each language matters more than usual.

**Check-In** is the small act of noting how you are right now. It should sound light, quick, and self-directed:
closer to "taking a moment to notice how I'm doing" than to something you are summoned to or have to complete.

**Entry** is the saved record that this act leaves behind, one point on the user's timeline. It should sound neutral
and quiet, like a mark on a calendar.

The two should stay recognizably different from each other. The app talks about doing a check-in and about looking at
entries, and people should be able to tell which is meant. If your language has one natural word for both, a small
difference in wording (an extra word, or a different one) is usually enough.

These are short English words that make a small gesture sound small. Your task is to find words that feel the same way
in your language, not to find a dictionary equivalent.

## Impressions to steer away from

Some everyday words in English app-speak carry an impression we don't want, even when they are technically accurate.
It is the impression that matters, not the word, so look at how the words in your language *feel*.

- **Being assessed.** Words that suggest a quiz, a test, or a score to pass make people feel they are being graded
  or could get it wrong. The app asks how you are, and nothing you say is correct or incorrect.
- **Being scheduled or treated.** Words like "session" suggest an appointment or a clinical setting, with someone
  waiting and watching. The app is a private moment, not an appointment.
- **Being given homework.** Words like "log" suggest admin, upkeep, or a duty you could fall behind on. The app
  should feel like a place to rest a thought, not another chore.

If a word that sounds like one of these is the most natural and gentle choice in your language, use it. These are
things to *weigh*, not words to avoid by reflex. What counts is how it lands, so if you're unsure, imagine reading it
at the end of a very bad day.

## Practical points

- **Placeholders** such as `{current}`, `{total}`, and `{score}` are filled in by the app. Keep them exactly as
  written. You can move them wherever your sentence needs them.
- **Built-in questions** are the heart of the app, so they deserve the most care. Phrase each one the way a
  thoughtful person would ask it, and keep the scale labels (the ends of the 1–5 range) neutral and relatable. Avoid
  anything that sounds like a diagnosis or a judgement. Don't turn "low" into a failure or "high" into a goal.
- **Length** matters on small screens and for large text settings. If your translation is much longer than the
  English, check whether a shorter one reads just as gently.
- **Don't translate word for word.** Rephrase freely. A natural sentence that carries the same warmth is better than
  a faithful one that sounds foreign.
- **Missing text is fine.** If you can't finish, anything left out falls back to English, so a partial translation
  still works. A thoughtful partial one is better than a complete one that rushes.

---

## When something doesn't fit

Languages differ, and so do cultures of care. Formal and informal address, how people talk about moods, and which
words feel clinical or cold can all vary a lot. You know your language and your readers better than this guide does.
If following it would make your translation worse, follow your judgment, and add a sentence about it to your pull
request or alongside your locale so the next translator knows why.
